N1About

I'm a physician and an AI product manager at an academic health system. Before that I spent six years building inside the EHR as a physician builder, which is where I learned that most clinical software fails in the workflow, not in the code.

Today I evaluate AI vendors, design governance, build agentic workflows, and teach clinicians how these tools work. I'm completing a Master of Decision Analytics at VCU.

What I keep asking is less "does it work?" than "why does it work, and where does that stop being true?"

N2Work

Open work

FAPM · v0.1 · specification + checker

Feature-Annotated Process Models

A way to specify a clinical AI workflow so the governance committee, the engineers and the auditor are reading the same document. Each step declares what it needs, sees, reads and produces.

Node N = { F_required, F_available, F_consumed, F_produced }

Run on an ambient scribe workflow, the checker finds six gaps before go-live. One of them: no step defines which version of the note actually gets signed.
N3Convictions
  • Deployed is not validated. Validated is not adopted.Three stages, three sets of requirements. Go-live is where the work starts.
  • Workflow first, technology second.A tool inherits every weakness of the workflow it lands in.
  • Literacy before tools.A governance framework is only as good as the people who can perform it.
  • Champions are the mechanism.Adoption spreads, or stalls, through the clinicians you ask to lead it.
  • AI belongs on the care team, not in place of it.Pattern recognition at population scale, feeding human judgment in the gray zone.
